A splat in the flow of a page

Scroll this page with the mouse over the viewer below: the page keeps scrolling. Drag to orbit. Click inside first and the wheel zooms instead; move the pointer away and scrolling returns to the page.

1. Custom element, loads when it nears the viewport

Filler

This section only exists to put distance between the players, so the ones below are well outside the viewport when the page opens. They should not download anything or create a WebGL context until you scroll near them, and the one above should stop rendering once it is out of view.

2. A gallery tile that waits for a click

For large captures: only the poster is fetched until the visitor asks for the 3D view.

3. JavaScript API, transparent, themed by the page

Created with new SplatPlayer(element, options) and destroyed with player.destroy(), the way a React effect would. The canvas is transparent, so the page's surface color shows through, and the controls take the page's ink and accent colors.

4. A component singled out and labelled

A section box marks out one part of the splat. Here the box carries its own label, a name above and a description below, and the rest of the splat is faded rather than removed. A second label is pinned to a point. Labels are plain text with no background. The two buttons at the lower right switch the labels and the cut.

5. Set flowing

A force field moves the splat's own points: they leave their place, travel, fade and start again from home, keeping their shape and color. Nothing is added. This one is a swirl with a little lift. The wave button stills it; viewers who ask their system for reduced motion get it stilled to begin with.

6. Animated

A scene can carry a keyframed animation, made on the editor's timeline. Here the camera travels round the knot while the swirl builds and dies away. It plays by itself; the bar at the bottom pauses and scrubs it. Take hold of the view and the camera stops for you, and the play button hands it back.
